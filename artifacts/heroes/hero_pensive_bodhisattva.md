# Pensive Bodhisattva

## Why this matters

For visitors new to Korean art, this is the quietest possible monument: not a ruler, not a weapon, but concentration made visible.

## What you're looking at

The bodhisattva sits with one leg folded over the other and one hand lifted toward the cheek, a posture associated with contemplative images of Maitreya. The body is spare rather than ornamental: a simple crown, exposed torso, and balanced proportions keep attention on the face and hand.

## The story

Such pensive images moved across East Asia with Buddhism, but Korea gave the type an unusually inward mood. National Treasure No. 83 is often compared with the red-pine bodhisattva at Kōryūji in Kyoto, a reminder that sculpture, belief, and technique crossed the sea long before modern borders.
